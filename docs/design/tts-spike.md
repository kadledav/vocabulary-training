# M6 spike: Kokoro with sherpa-onnx 2.0.1 from Rust

Date: 2026-11-21. Scope: design §17 row M6 ("spike first"), §10. Result: the
design's choice works; three findings change details of §10 (below).

## Setup

- Machine: Apple M5 Pro (arm64), measured natively on macOS and in a
  Podman 6.1.4 VM (linux/arm64, 7 vCPUs, 5.5 GiB) running the final image
  hardened as in Compose (read-only, `cap_drop ALL`, 2 CPUs, 1280 MB).
  amd64 is measured by the CI smoke test (`tools/tts-smoke.py`), not here.
- sherpa-onnx 2.0.1 (crates `sherpa-onnx`/`sherpa-onnx-sys` 2.0.1,
  2026-10-27; static libraries of the GitHub release v2.0.1, 2026-10-26),
  model `kokoro-multi-lang-v1_0` (fp32, sha256 `60703707…`) and
  `kokoro-int8-multi-lang-v1_0` (sha256 `72c34723…`), both uploaded
  2026-10-26; all checksums matched GitHub's asset digests.
- 2 inference threads (`VOCAB_TTS_THREADS=2`), voice `af_heart` (speaker 3)
  unless noted; `bf_emma` is speaker 21 (model metadata `speaker_names`).
- Texts: synthetic English sentences (3–40 words), single words, numbers,
  invented words; one 598-character text (the API maximum is 600).

## Decisions

- **fp32** (`model.onnx`), not int8 (changed in v0.2.1 for memory: int8,
  see "Small memory" below): int8 is 2.4× slower on this CPU (dynamic
  quantisation kernels) for 0.2 GB less memory; fp32 is well inside the
  design threshold. On x86 with VNNI int8 might be faster; the CI smoke
  test on amd64 shows the time per clip in the log (`synthesis_ms`), and the
  switch is a Dockerfile change (file name) only.
- **Voices**: `af_heart` (US, default) and `bf_emma` (UK), as designed.
- **Limits** (Compose, smoke test): memory 1280 MB without swap (peak
  0.91 GB + 40 %), 2 CPUs, 64 PIDs; request timeout 60 s (the longest text
  takes ~11 s here).
- **MP3 encoder**: LAME 3.100 through `mp3lame-encoder` 0.2.5 /
  `mp3lame-sys` 0.1.11 (bundled source, built with autotools; LAME is
  LGPL-2.0-or-later, the Rust bindings LGPL-3.0), VBR quality 5, mono
  24 kHz, with a Xing/LAME tag frame so players know the exact duration.

## Findings that change the design (§10)

1. **sherpa-onnx 2.0 has no espeak-ng any more** (upstream PR 4055: "sherpa-onnx
   is GPL-free"). Kokoro's front-end is lexicon-only: every word not in the
   lexicon is **silently dropped** (in the spike: "1990", "3rd" and invented
   words; "dogs" and "wanted" are not in the US lexicon either) and
   **printed to stderr** ("OOV word skipped:
   '…'") — words of book sentences would land in the container log (R-24).
   Also, one engine instance merges the US and UK lexicons (last wins), so
   the UK voice would not get UK pronunciations (R-42).
   → `vocab-tts` does grapheme-to-phoneme itself (`phonemize` module): the
   lexicon of the voice's accent (Kokoro v1.0's misaki lexicons, 178k US /
   192k UK entries), numbers/years/ordinals read as words, accents folded,
   possessive and regular suffixes (-s, -ed, -ing, -ly) derived from the
   stem with the right ending sound, hyphenated parts, and letter-by-letter
   spelling as the last resort (counted in the log as `spelled_out`). The
   engine gets only phoneme code points (`GenerationConfig.phoneme_codepoints`);
   nothing it logs can contain text. Same speed and duration as the
   engine's own front-end in the spike.
2. **The image is not a GPL-3.0 combined work.** Without espeak-ng (and
   without copying the archive's `espeak-ng-data`) the image is
   `MIT AND Apache-2.0 AND BSD-3-Clause AND Unicode-3.0 AND LGPL-3.0-only
   AND LGPL-2.0-or-later` (LAME and its bindings are the only copyleft
   parts). `/licenses` carries those texts, `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt` (generated by `tools/licenses.py`) and `SOURCE.md`
   instead of the GPL text; the label says the same. The repository stays MIT.
3. **Loudness**: scaling the whole clip down to the −1.5 dBTP ceiling (design
   §10.3 step 6) leaves sentences at −18 … −20 LUFS (speech has a high crest
   factor). `postprocess` uses a look-ahead peak limiter (5 ms look-ahead,
   80 ms release) with make-up passes instead; the scaling remains as a final
   safety net. Result: every clip −16.1 … −16.9 LUFS.

## Other differences to §10.2 (from the M6 phase 1 brief)

- API `POST /v1/speak` (not `/v1/synthesize`), **no token**: the container is
  only on the `internal: true` network shared with the app (reasoning in
  design §10.2 and §13); every input is validated anyway. Text:
  invisible formatting characters are removed (a soft hyphen joins its
  word), every whitespace character (tab, CR, line breaks) becomes a
  space; then ≤ 600 characters, no control characters, no `<`/`>` — no
  SSML (400 `invalid_text`); no run of more than 64 non-space characters
  (422 `token_too_long`) or 15 digits (422 `number_too_long`); at most 900
  phoneme symbols, about 60 s of speech (422 `too_long_to_speak`). Voice
  from the list; speed 0.5–1.5; JSON object with exactly those fields;
  body ≤ 4 KiB. Responses carry `X-Duration-Ms`, `X-Loudness-Lufs`,
  `X-Voice` and `X-Approximate`; `422 nothing_to_say` when nothing in the
  text is speakable, `503 busy` (with `Retry-After`) when one synthesis runs
  and four wait, `503 timeout` after 60 s — the abandoned work is cancelled
  (skipped before the engine, stopped between chunks inside it). Chunks
  never exceed 300 phoneme symbols, even for one long word (Kokoro's
  context is 510). `GET /v1/voices`, `GET /healthz`.
- Configuration `VOCAB_TTS_LISTEN` (default `0.0.0.0:8090`),
  `VOCAB_TTS_MODEL_DIR` (`/model`), `VOCAB_TTS_THREADS` (2),
  `VOCAB_TTS_LOG_LEVEL` (info); unknown `VOCAB_TTS_*` = startup error. At
  start the service speaks "Hello." with both voices (self-test) before it
  listens.
- The clip identity in the app includes the front-end and engine versions
  (design §10.3 step 2), so a front-end change regenerates the clips (the
  old ones play until their replacement is ready).

## Building

`vocab-tts` is a workspace member but not a default member. Its engine
module (`src/engine/kokoro.rs`) and its only dependency on sherpa-onnx exist
only with `RUSTFLAGS="--cfg vocab_tts_engine"` (a target-specific
dependency on that cfg). `sherpa-onnx-sys` links the static libraries and,
**without `SHERPA_ONNX_LIB_DIR`, its build script downloads them from
GitHub without a checksum**; without the cfg it is not even compiled, so:

- Plain `cargo build/clippy/test --workspace` (any features) works offline
  without the libraries; such a service binary refuses to start ("this
  build has no TTS engine"). All tests run with a fake engine.
- `docker/Dockerfile.tts` sets the cfg and `SHERPA_ONNX_LIB_DIR` (archives
  added with `ADD --checksum`); `--target test` runs the tests linked
  against the real libraries.
- CI (`rust` job) also runs clippy on `vocab-tts` with the cfg and an empty
  `SHERPA_ONNX_LIB_DIR` (checks the engine code, links nothing).
- Locally with the engine: download the archive of your platform from the
  v2.0.1 release, compare `shasum -a 256` with GitHub's digest (macOS arm64
  `sherpa-onnx-v2.0.1-osx-arm64-static-lib.tar.bz2`
  `d7a9b14f337958e7373c8c650f046df33d210bbaff2f33cb8322dda663ee5e1c`),
  unpack it, then `SHERPA_ONNX_LIB_DIR=<dir>/lib RUSTFLAGS="--cfg
  vocab_tts_engine" cargo build -p vocab-tts`.
- `deny.toml` has no target filter (a target list cannot see the cfg), so
  cargo-deny checks the engine's crates too.

## Pronunciation coverage (phase 2)

Word lookup order: Kokoro lexicon of the accent → regular inflection of a
lexicon stem → CMUdict (cmusphinx/cmudict at `74790861`, BSD-2-Clause,
converted from ARPAbet to misaki per accent, `src/phonemize/arpabet.rs`)
→ inflection of a CMUdict stem → two known words written together
("mashup") → letter by letter. The API returns `X-Approximate` (words from
CMUdict, a compound split, or spelled) and the app stores it per clip.

| List (texts, US / UK) | before: lexicons + inflections | after: + CMUdict + compounds |
|---|---|---|
| synthetic uncommon words, phrasal verbs, compounds (342, `tests/data/uncommon-words.txt`) | 6 / 3 spelled | 4 / 0 spelled (7 / 3 approximate) |
| owner's export: headwords (74) | 0 / 1 spelled | 0 / 0 spelled (0 / 1 approximate) |
| owner's export: `speak` texts (74; 1 differs from its headword) | 0 / 1 | 0 / 0 |
| owner's export: forms (148) | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 |
| owner's export: sentences (223) | 29 / 31 with a spelled word | 7 / 7 with a spelled word |

The owner's only out-of-lexicon headword was "potluck" (UK lexicon only;
now from CMUdict, marked approximate). The sentences' remaining spelled
words are the book's invented names and an all-caps acronym (letter by
letter is right for the acronym). Still spelled in the synthetic list (US):
"frisson", "pococurante", "tatterdemalion", "cack-handed" (its part
"cack"). The owner's data was read locally for these counts only; nothing
of it is in the repository.

## Small memory (2026-11-21, v0.2.1)

The owner's home server has little free RAM; the 0.2.0 container kept the
fp32 model loaded all the time (567 MiB idle, up to 980 MiB). Decision:
the smallest setup that still speaks English well, same Kokoro voices and
pronunciations; speed does not matter (clips are made once, in the
background). Target: the whole app under 400 MB.

**Setup of the measurements:** the image in the Podman VM of the spike
(linux/arm64, Apple M5 Pro), hardened as in Compose, `--cpus 1` unless
noted; memory is the container's cgroup (`memory.current`/`memory.peak`,
`anon` of `memory.stat`), not in-process counters; texts synthetic (the
smoke test's sentences and words, the 598-character text, the worst
accepted texts). "Fresh" = a new container per text (one request after the
start). amd64 is not measured here (the CI smoke test prints its numbers).

### What each lever does

| Lever | Measured | Kept |
|---|---|---|

| Chunk length 150 / 100 / 75 / 50 symbols (fresh, int8, 1 thread, hash-map lexicons) | a sentence: 384 / 384 / 384 / 370 MiB; the 598-char text 524 / 524 / 411 / 432; compounds up to 600 characters 702 / 534 / 499 / 412; spelled 64-letter word 510 / 442 / 405 / 417 | 100 (a sentence of up to ~17 words stays one chunk; longer ones are cut at a pause); front-end `g2p-4` |
| One engine call per chunk instead of one for all | same memory (470 vs 474 MiB for the 598-char text), same audio duration (±1 ms) | needed for the next row |
| New ONNX Runtime session only before a longer chunk | no help: the 598-char text (sentence-long chunks) still 464 MiB | no |
| New session for every chunk, dropped after it | the 598-char text 464 → 406 MiB, compounds 474 → 413; +0.35 s per chunk (words 0.6 → 1.0 s, sentences +0.3 s) | yes |
| Engine process started on demand, ended after `VOCAB_TTS_IDLE_SECONDS` | container 1–3 MiB (anon ≤ 2 MiB) after the start self-test and after the idle time; the start self-test (both voices) takes ~1.5 s | yes (default 180 s) |
| ONNX Runtime options (arena, memory pattern, mmap of the model) | not in the public API of the pinned `sherpa-onnx` 2.0.1 (`OfflineTtsModelConfig` has threads, provider, debug only) | not available |
| Lexicons loaded per request, dropped before the engine runs | would save ~20 MiB of the peak for ~130 ms per clip | not done |
| glibc malloc tuning (`MALLOC_ARENA_MAX`, thresholds) | not measured | — |

Why so much more than the file: the int8 model file is 109 MiB, the loaded
model ~233 MiB of RSS (ONNX Runtime's initialised and pre-packed weights);
a chunk's working memory comes on top (60–150 MiB, growing with its length,
and with every further chunk a session runs: hence the fresh session).

### Final setup

int8 model, 1 thread (`cpus: 1`), chunks of ≤ 100 symbols, a fresh session
per chunk, an engine process that ends 180 s after the last request,
`mem_limit` 512 MB (peak 415 MiB + 23 %; no swap). Above the limit the
kernel kills only the engine process: that clip fails as a crash
(`500 engine_crashed`, three of them and the app marks it "crashed the
audio service"), the service stays up.

| Idle (after the start, and after the idle time) | 567 MiB | 1–3 MiB (anon ≤ 2 MiB) |

| Peak: everything, worst accepted texts included | 980 MiB | 415 MiB |
| Image (linux/arm64) | 437 MB | 226 MB |

| Time, 1 thread (int8) | `--cpus 1` (= unrestricted for one thread) | `--cpus 0.5` | 0.2.0, 2 threads |
|---|---|---|---|

300 clips (100 words, 200 sentences): ~10 min at one core here, ~22 min at
half a core; if a NAS core is 3–5 times slower (not measured), 30–60 min
at one core.

Two containers (app and TTS under the Compose limits, one import of 30
synthetic words with three sentences each, 120 clips, `VOCAB_TTS_IDLE_SECONDS=30`):
all 120 clips ready, none failed, one engine process for all of them; the
TTS container peaked at 399 MiB (sampled every second: at most 372 MiB),
the app container at 21 MiB (during the import; 6–7 MiB otherwise). Idle
before and 30 s after the last clip: TTS 1 MiB, app 7 MiB. So the whole
app is far under 400 MB while idle, and about 405–420 MiB (425–440 MB)
at the peak of clip making: under 400 MB is not reached while clips are
being made. (The run shared the CPU with a build, so its 11 minutes are no
speed figure.)

Samples (6 sentences, 6 words, US and UK voice; fp32 of 0.2.0 vs. this
setup; ffmpeg on the decoded MP3s): durations equal within ±0.1 s except
the one 21-word sentence that is now cut at its comma (±0.3 s); loudness
−16.0 … −16.9 LUFS (0.2.0) and −16.0 … −16.8 LUFS (0.2.1); no clipping
(highest sample −1.6 dBFS, highest true peak −1.4 dBTP; 0.2.0: −1.5 dBFS,
−1.2 dBTP). Whether int8 sounds as good is the owner's call (the samples
are outside the repository).

### Timeouts

The longest accepted text takes ~20 s here at one thread and ~40 s at half
a CPU (`--cpus 0.5`); a NAS core may be several times slower, and the first
request after an idle time loads lexicons and model first. So: the
service's request timeout is 600 s (was 60), the app waits 630 s for a clip
(was 75) and a timeout costs the clip an attempt (retried 30 s … 24 h
later), never a crash; the engine process gets 120 s to load; a crash is
only an engine process that died (`engine_crashed`) or a connection that
broke. The 2 GiB clip cache cap, input limits, long-token and crash-loop
protections are unchanged.
